## TOPIC 1 - SIMPLE PAST

### The Simple Past is used to talk about:

- *Completed actions in the past*
- *Past experiences*
- *Finished events*
- *Actions that happened at a specific time*

**Structure**

**Affirmative**
- *Subject + Verb (Past) + Complement*
- *Example:*
- *I worked yesterday.*
- *She answered the customer's email.*
- *They visited the office last week.*
---

**Negative**
- *Subject + did not (didn't) + Base Verb*
- *Example:*
- *I didn't work on Saturday.*
- *He didn't answer the phone.*
- *We didn't receive the payment.*
---

**Question**
- *Did + Subject + Base Verb?*
- *Example:*
- *Did you call the customer?*
- *Did she finish the report?*
- *Did they receive the package?*

## TOPIC 2 - ED PRONUNCIATION

_For the regular verbs, the Simple Past is formed by adding -ED, and this has three different pronunciations._

- */ɪd/ - Used after verbs ending in T or D.*
- */t/ - Used after voiceless sounds.*
- */d/ - Used after voiced sounds.*

## TOPIC 3 - WH QUESTION WORDS

_WH- question words are special words used to ask for specific information. They cannot be answered with a simple "yes" or "no". They are used for **open questions**._

## TOPIC 4 - GERUNDS & INFINITIVES

_Gerunds and infinitives are forms of verbs that function as nouns. The **gerund** uses the ending **+ing** (like eating) and the **infinitive** uses the particle **to + base verb** (like to eat). They are chosen according to the grammatical rule of the sentence, the main verb that accompanies them, or whether they follow a preposition._

### When to use the gerund (-ing)

- *As the subject of a sentence when beginning to talk about an action in general:*
- *Example: Swimming is good for health.*
- *After prepositions:*
- *Example: She is good at drawing.*
- *After certain common verbs such as enjoy, avoid, finish or mind:*
- *Example: I enjoy reading.*
---

### When to use the infinitive (to + verb)

- *After adjectives:*
- *Example: It is nice to meet you.*
- *To express a purpose or reason for doing something:*
- *Example: I went to the store to buy milk.*
- *After certain common verbs such as want, need, hope or decide:*
- *Example: I want to learn.*
---

### Common Verbs Followed by Gerunds
- *Enjoy*
- *Finish*
- *Avoid*
- *Suggest*
- *Keep*
- *Practice*
- *Consider*
- *Mind*

**Example:**
- *I enjoy helping customers.*
- *She finished writing the report.*
- *They suggested changing the appointment.*
---

### Common Verbs Followed by Infinitives
- *Want*
- *Need*
- *Decide*
- *Plan*
- *Hope*
- *Learn*
- *Promise*
- *Agree*

**Example:**
- *I want to speak with the manager.*
- *She decided to accept the offer.*
- *We need to verify your identity.*
---

### Verbs That Can Use Both

_Some verbs can be followed by either a gerund or an infinitive._

- *Start*
- *Begin*
- *Like*
- *Love*
- *Hate*
- *Continue*

**Example:**
- *I started working at 8:00.*
- *I started to work at 8:00.*

_Both sentences are correct._
