## 🔑Topic 1: Make vs. Do

{{< img src="images/do_vs_make.png" alt="Do vs. Make" >}}

Although both verbs can be translated as *hacer* in Spanish, they are used in different situations.

### Use "Make"

Use **make** when you create, produce, build, prepare, or cause something.

Examples:

* I make breakfast every morning.
* She made a cake yesterday.
* They make furniture.
* He made a mistake.
* We made a decision.

**Common Expressions with Make**

* Make a phone call
* Make a plan
* Make a decision
* Make money
* Make friends
* Make breakfast
* Make dinner
* Make a reservation
* Make progress
* Make a mistake

### Use "Do"

Use **do** for activities, jobs, work, tasks, or responsibilities.

Examples:

* I do my homework every day.
* She does the laundry.
* They do their job well.
* He is doing the dishes.
* We do business together.

**Common Expressions with Do**

* Do homework
* Do housework
* Do the dishes
* Do exercise
* Do business
* Do your job
* Do your best
* Do research
* Do the laundry
* Do a favor

## 🔑Topic 2: Adverbs of Manner

### What are Adverbs of Manner?

Adverbs of manner describe **how** an action is performed.

Most adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective.

Examples:

| **Adjective** | **Adverb** |
| --- | --- |
| Quick | Quickly |
| Slow | Slowly |
| Careful | Carefully |
| Easy | Easily |
| Happy | Happily |
| Loud | Loudly |
| Quiet | Quietly |

### Sentence Structure

**Subject + Verb + Adverb**

Examples:

* She speaks clearly.
* He drives carefully.
* They work efficiently.
* We answered politely.
* The children played happily.

Some adverbs do not end in **-ly**.

Examples:

* Fast
* Hard
* Well
* Late
* Early

Examples:

* He runs fast.
* She works hard.
* They speak well.
* I arrived early.

## 🔑Topic 3: Rather and Rather Than

These expressions are used to show preferences.

### Rather

Use **rather** to mean **preferably**.

Examples:

* I would rather stay home.
* She would rather study tonight.
* We'd rather eat at home.
* He would rather drive than fly.

Structure:

**Subject + would rather + base verb**

### Rather Than

Use **rather than** to compare two options or show preference for one over another.

Examples:

* I would rather walk than drive.
* She chose tea rather than coffee.
* We decided to stay home rather than go out.
* He works during the day rather than at night.

Structure:

**Subject + would rather + verb + than + verb**

or

**Rather than + noun/verb**

## 🔑Topic 4: Neither and Either

These words are commonly used to express agreement or disagreement.

### Neither

Use **neither** when agreeing with a negative statement.

Examples:

* I don't like coffee.
* Neither do I.

More examples:

* She doesn't work on weekends.
* Neither does he.
* They aren't busy today.
* Neither are we.

### Either

Use **either** in negative sentences to mean **also not**.

Examples:

* I don't like coffee either.
* She doesn't eat meat either.
* We don't have time either.
* He can't come either.

### Difference Between Neither and Either

| **Neither** | **Either** |
| --- | --- |
| Used to agree with a negative statement. | Used at the end of a negative sentence. |
| Neither do I. | I don't like it either. |
| Neither can she. | She can't come either. |
