## 🔑Topic 1: Past Perfect

### What is the Past Perfect?

The **Past Perfect** describes an action that happened **before another action or moment in the past**.

It helps show the order of past events.

### Structure

**Affirmative**

Subject + **had + past participle**

Examples:

* I had finished my work before lunch.
* She had left when I arrived.
* They had already eaten before the meeting started.
* We had studied English before moving abroad.

**Negative**

Subject + **had not (hadn't) + past participle**

Examples:

* I hadn't seen the movie before.
* She hadn't finished the report.
* They hadn't met before the conference.

**Questions**

Had + Subject + Past Participle?

Examples:

* Had you finished your homework?
* Had she called before you left?
* Had they arrived on time?

### When Do We Use the Past Perfect?

* To show which past action happened first.
* To describe completed actions before another event.
* To tell stories more clearly.

Examples:

* By the time we arrived, the movie had started.
* He had forgotten his keys before leaving home.
* They had never traveled abroad until last year.

## 🔑Topic 2: Word Order in Questions

English questions follow a specific word order.

### Yes/No Questions

**Auxiliary Verb + Subject + Main Verb**

Examples:

* Do you work here?
* Did she call you?
* Have they finished?
* Is he at home?
* Can you help me?

### WH-Questions

**WH-word + Auxiliary + Subject + Main Verb**

Examples:

* Where do you live?
* What did she buy?
* Why are they late?
* When have you finished your work?
* Who called you?

### Common Question Words

* What
* Where
* When
* Why
* Who
* Which
* How
* How long
* How often
* How much
* How many

### Tips

Remember:

English questions almost always require an **auxiliary verb** before the subject.

Incorrect:

* You like coffee?

Correct:

* Do you like coffee?

## 🔑Topic 3: Third Conditional

### What is the Third Conditional?

The **Third Conditional** is used to talk about situations that **did not happen in the past** and to imagine a different result.

It often expresses regret, criticism, or missed opportunities.

### Structure

**If + Past Perfect, would have + past participle**

Examples:

* If I had studied more, I would have passed the exam.
* If she had left earlier, she would have caught the bus.
* If we had known, we would have helped.
* If they had practiced more, they would have won.

### More Examples

* If I had remembered your birthday, I would have called you.
* If he had saved more money, he would have bought a new car.
* If it hadn't rained, we would have gone to the beach.
* If they had listened carefully, they wouldn't have made that mistake.

### When Do We Use It?

* Talking about regrets.
* Imagining different outcomes.
* Discussing missed opportunities.
* Reflecting on past decisions.

## 🔑Topic 4: Order of Adjectives

### What is the Order of Adjectives?

When using more than one adjective before a noun, English follows a natural order.

The correct order is:

* Opinion
* Size
* Age
* Shape
* Color
* Origin
* Material
* Purpose
* Noun

### Examples

* A beautiful small old round red Italian wooden dining table.
* A nice big blue car.
* An expensive new French watch.
* A small black leather bag.
* A lovely young Mexican teacher.

### Common Adjective Categories

**Opinion**

* Beautiful
* Nice
* Amazing
* Interesting
* Expensive

**Size**

* Big
* Small
* Large
* Tiny
* Tall

**Age**

* New
* Old
* Young
* Ancient
* Modern

**Shape**

* Round
* Square
* Long
* Short
* Flat

**Color**

* Red
* Blue
* Green
* White
* Black

**Origin**

* American
* Mexican
* Canadian
* Italian
* Japanese

**Material**

* Wooden
* Plastic
* Metal
* Leather
* Cotton

**Purpose**

These adjectives explain what something is used for.

Examples:

* Sleeping bag
* Coffee cup
* Running shoes
* Dining table
* Tennis racket
