## 🔑Topic 1: Relative Clauses

### What are Relative Clauses?

Relative clauses give additional information about a noun without starting a new sentence.

They usually begin with a **relative pronoun**.

The most common relative pronouns are:

* Who
* Whom
* Which
* That
* Whose
* Where
* When

### Who

Use **who** to refer to people.

Examples:

* The woman who works here is very friendly.
* I met a customer who speaks French.
* She has a brother who lives in Canada.

### Whom

Use **whom** as the object of the sentence. It is more common in formal English.

Examples:

* The client whom we contacted yesterday replied today.
* The person whom you met is my manager.

### Which

Use **which** to refer to animals or things.

Examples:

* The computer which I bought is very fast.
* The book which you recommended was excellent.
* The restaurant which opened last month is very popular.

### That

Use **that** to refer to people or things in defining clauses.

Examples:

* The employee that helped me was very polite.
* This is the phone that I ordered.
* I have a friend that lives in Texas.

### Whose

Use **whose** to show possession.

Examples:

* The man whose car was stolen called the police.
* I know a teacher whose students love English.
* She met a woman whose daughter is a doctor.

### Where

Use **where** to refer to places.

Examples:

* This is the office where I work.
* The city where I grew up is very small.

### When

Use **when** to refer to time.

Examples:

* I remember the day when we first met.
* Summer is the season when many people travel.

## 🔑Topic 2: Reported Speech

### What is Reported Speech?

Reported Speech is used to tell someone what another person said without using their exact words.

### Direct Speech

Examples:

* Sarah said, "I am tired."
* John said, "I work from home."
* The customer said, "I need help."

### Reported Speech

Examples:

* Sarah said that she was tired.
* John said that he worked from home.
* The customer said that he needed help.

### Common Changes in Reported Speech

| **Direct Speech** | **Reported Speech** |
| --- | --- |
| am/is | was |
| are | were |
| do/does | did |
| will | would |
| can | could |
| have/has | had |

### Examples

Direct:

* "I will call you."

Reported:

* She said that she would call me.

Direct:

* "I can help."

Reported:

* He said that he could help.

Direct:

* "We are busy."

Reported:

* They said that they were busy.

## 🔑Topic 3: Giving an Opinion Vocabulary

There are many polite ways to express your opinion in English.

### Common Expressions

* I think...
* I believe...
* In my opinion...
* From my point of view...
* As far as I'm concerned...
* Personally...
* I would say...
* It seems to me that...
* I feel that...
* I agree that...

### Examples

* I think this is a great idea.
* In my opinion, teamwork is very important.
* Personally, I prefer online classes.
* I believe communication is the key to success.
* From my point of view, customer service should always come first.

### Agreeing

* I agree.
* Absolutely.
* You're right.
* That's true.
* I think so too.
* Exactly.

Examples:

* I agree with your idea.
* That's absolutely right.
* Exactly! I was thinking the same thing.

### Disagreeing Politely

* I don't think so.
* I'm not sure about that.
* I see your point, but...
* I respectfully disagree.
* That's one way to look at it.
* I have a different opinion.

Examples:

* I see your point, but I think we need more information.
* I'm not sure I agree with that decision.
* I respectfully disagree because there is another solution.

## 🔑Topic 4: Negotiation Vocabulary

Negotiation is an important communication skill in business and customer service.

### Common Negotiation Vocabulary

* Agreement
* Offer
* Counteroffer
* Proposal
* Deal
* Discount
* Budget
* Price
* Contract
* Terms
* Conditions
* Deadline
* Payment
* Client
* Supplier
* Partnership
* Compromise
* Bargain
* Profit
* Value

### Useful Negotiation Expressions

* Can we negotiate the price?
* What is your best offer?
* We can offer a discount.
* That sounds reasonable.
* I'm afraid that's too expensive.
* Could you lower the price?
* We have reached an agreement.
* Let's find a solution that works for both of us.
* I understand your position.
* We are willing to compromise.

### Examples

* The client accepted our proposal.
* We reached a successful agreement.
* They offered a better price.
* Our company signed the contract yesterday.
* Both sides agreed on the final terms.
