## Key Concepts(Grammar Rules)
*The Temperature:*

To talk about temperature in English, we usually say the number + degrees + the temperature scale (Celsius or Fahrenheit).

*Structure*

_Number + Degrees + Celcius/Fahrenheit_

*Examples:*

* _25°C → Twenty-five degrees Celsius_
* _30°C → Thirty degrees Celsius_
* _72°F → Seventy-two degrees Fahrenheit_
* _90°F → Ninety degrees Fahrenheit_

**Note:**
 In the United States, people usually use Fahrenheit, in most other countries, people use Celsius.

*Dates:*
In American English, dates are usually said using the month + the ordinal number of the day + the year. Keep in mind that the month goes first unlike the Spanish language where we usually say the day first.

*Structure:*
**MM + DD + YYYY**

*Examples:*

* _March 1st, 2001 → March first, two thousand one_
* _April 10th, 1996 → April tenth, nineteen ninety-six_
* _July 23rd, 1905 → July twenty-third, nineteen oh five_
* _December 31st, 1900 → December thirty-first, nineteen hundred_

**Note:**
We ONLY use ordinal numbers for the day (first, second, third, fourth, etc.) avoid using cardinal numbers.

*How to Read Years in English:*
1. Years that end in 00 are read as “hundreds.” Examples
    * 1800 → eighteen hundred
    * 1900 → nineteen hundred
2. Years from 1910 to 1999 are usually read in pairs. Example:
    * 1996 → nineteen ninety-six
    * 1984 → nineteen eighty-four
3. Years from 2000 to 2009 are usually read as a full number. Examples:
    * 2003 → two thousand three
    * 2009 → two thousand nine
5. From 2010 to the present, years are commonly read in pairs again. Examples:
    * 2013 → twenty thirteen
    * 2026 → twenty twenty-six

#### ✔️ Tip: Both two thousand thirteen and twenty thirteen are correct, but “twenty thirteen” is more common in conversation.

## 📝 Summary:

_Use the following structure to read a date: MM – DD – YYYY_
1. The month goes first.
2. The day is read with ordinal numbers only.
3. Each year has its own reading structure, memorizing works better in this case.
