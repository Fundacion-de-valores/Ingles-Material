## 📖 Introduction

Today you will learn the difference between a **gerund** (verb + *-ing*) and an **infinitive** (*to* + verb). You will explore the different rules for when to use each one, and discover 4 special verbs that change their meaning depending on which form follows them.

---

## 📚 Important Terms — Daily Vocabulary

| Term | Definition |
|------|------------|
| **Gerund** | A verb ending in *-ing* used as a noun (*running, eating, sleeping*) |
| **Infinitive** | *To* + base verb (*to run, to sing, to speak*) |
| **Zero Infinitive** | Base verb without *to* — used after auxiliaries (*do, does, did*) |
| **Preposition** | A word like *at, for, about, in* — always followed by a gerund |
| **Object Pronoun** | *me, you, him, her, us, them* — always followed by an infinitive |

---

## 🔑 Key Concepts — Detailed Explanation

### 🔵 1. Gerunds

A **gerund** is a verb that ends in *-ing* and functions as a noun.

---

#### 1.1 Gerunds After Some Verbs

When two verbs appear in the same sentence, the second verb must be a gerund or infinitive. These verbs are **always followed by a gerund**:

> *enjoy, admit, advise, appreciate, deny, miss, mention, mind, avoid, consider, finish, keep, suggest, recommend*

| Example |
|---------|
| *I **enjoy swimming**.* |
| *I don't **mind helping** you.* |
| *He **denied stealing** the money.* |

---

#### 1.2 Gerunds After Prepositions

When a **preposition** (*at, for, about, in, on, of…*) is followed by a verb, that verb **must be a gerund**.

| Example |
|---------|
| *She's good **at swimming**.* |
| *He apologized **for being** late.* |
| *Thank you **for coming**!* |

---

#### 1.3 Gerunds as the Subject of a Sentence

When an **action becomes the subject** of a sentence, use a gerund.

| Example |
|---------|
| ***Swimming** is good for your health.* |
| ***Reading** is my favorite hobby.* |
| ***Speaking** English is easier than you think.* |

> 💡 **Negative gerund:** use **not + -ing**
> *He enjoys **not having** homework.*
> ***Not smoking** is essential for your health.*

---

### 🟢 2. Infinitives

An **infinitive** is *to* + base verb.

---

#### 2.1 Infinitives After Some Verbs

These verbs are **always followed by an infinitive**:

> *want, agree, choose, decide, offer, promise, plan, hope, need, expect, learn, refuse, would like*

| Example |
|---------|
| *He **wanted to go** to the movies.* |
| *I **promise to be** there.* |
| *He **chose to leave** the meeting early.* |

---

#### 2.2 Infinitives After Object Pronouns

When a verb follows an **object pronoun** (*me, you, him, her, us, them*), use an infinitive.

| Example |
|---------|
| *I need **you to help** me.* |
| *Marta asked **Sam to buy** more milk.* |
| *She ordered **me to study**.* |

---

#### 2.3 Infinitives After Adjectives

When a verb follows an **adjective**, use an infinitive.

| Example |
|---------|
| *It's easy **to learn** English.* |
| *He's sorry **to be** rude to people.* |
| *Is it complicated **to drive** a bus?* |

> 💡 **Negative infinitive:** use **not to + verb**
> *She decided **not to go** to the party.*
> *He asked me **not to tell** anyone his secret.*
> *It's difficult **not to eat** fast food.*

---

#### 2.4 Zero Infinitive (Infinitive Without "To")

Use the **base verb without "to"** after the auxiliary verbs *do, does, did* in negatives and questions.

| Example |
|---------|
| *He didn't **say** anything.* |
| *Does Tim **work** with you?* |
| *I don't **believe** you.* |

---

### 🟡 3. Verbs That Change Meaning

These **4 verbs** change meaning depending on whether a gerund or infinitive follows: **regret, remember, forget, stop**.

---

#### Regret

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **regret + gerund** | Sorry about something done **in the past** | *I regret **telling** you about the competition.* |
| **regret + infinitive** | Sorry about something you are **about to say** (formal) | *We regret **to inform** you that your application was denied.* |

---

#### Remember / Forget

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **remember/forget + gerund** | Remembering/forgetting something that **already happened** | *I still remember **being** nervous on my first day of school.* / *I'll never forget **seeing** his face.* |
| **remember/forget + infinitive** | Remembering/forgetting **to do** something (a task) | *Remember **to call** your mother tonight!* / *I forgot **to bring** my lunch.* |

---

#### Stop

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|------|---------|---------|
| **stop + gerund** | You are **no longer doing** something | *I've stopped **buying** coffee — it's too expensive.* |
| **stop + infinitive** | You **interrupt** one action **in order to do** another | *I stopped **to buy** a coffee on the way to work.* (I stopped walking in order to buy it) |

---

## 💡 Examples

### Gerunds
> *"She isn't good **at painting**."* (preposition)
> ***"Doing** exercise is good for your health."* (subject)
> *"I love **going** to the cinema."* (after verb *love*)

### Infinitives
> *"Do you want **to go** on a trip with me?"* (after verb *want*)
> *"She asked me **to help** her with her homework."* (after object pronoun)
> *"It's difficult **to think** with a lot of noise around."* (after adjective)

Choose the correct form:

### Exercise 3 — Verbs That Change Meaning
Choose the correct meaning:

> *"I stopped **smoking**."* → I am **no longer smoking**.
> *"I stopped **to smoke**."* → I **interrupted** something else to smoke.
> *"I remember **locking** the door."* → I remember a **past action**.
> *"Remember **to lock** the door."* → Don't forget **to do** it.

## 📝 Summary — Key Points to Remember

> **Before you go, make sure you know:**

1. **Gerund** = verb + *-ing* — used after certain verbs, after prepositions, and as the subject of a sentence.
2. **Infinitive** = *to* + base verb — used after certain verbs, after object pronouns, and after adjectives.
3. **Zero Infinitive** = base verb (no *to*) — used after *do / does / did* in negatives and questions.
4. **Verbs that change meaning** — *regret, remember, forget, stop* mean something different with a gerund vs. an infinitive.

| | Gerund | Infinitive |
|-|--------|-----------|
| **After verbs** | enjoy, mind, admit, deny… | want, decide, promise, hope… |
| **After prepositions** | good **at** swimming ✅ | — |
| **As subject** | **Swimming** is healthy ✅ | — |
| **After object pronouns** | — | I need **you to help** ✅ |
| **After adjectives** | — | It's easy **to learn** ✅ |

> ⚠️ **Negative forms:**
> Gerund → **not + -ing** (*not smoking*)
> Infinitive → **not to + verb** (*decided not to go*)
