Topics of the Day: #

  • Simple Future
  • Time Expressions
  • Prepositions of Time (Review)

Learning Objectives: #

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  1. Use the simple future tense in affirmative, negative, and question forms to talk about plans, predictions, and decisions.
  2. Identify and use common time expressions for the future (e.g., tomorrow, next week, later) to give more detail about when actions will happen, in preparation for the call center experience.
  3. Apply prepositions of time (in, on, at) correctly when talking about future events and schedules.

Introduction: #

Today you will learn how to talk about things that haven’t happened yet by forming sentences in different ways, including statements, negatives, and questions. You will also practice adding details about when something will happen using common future time phrases, and review how to choose the correct time prepositions to make your ideas clear and accurate.

Key Concepts (Grammar Rules): #

The Simple Future: There are two words that we use in English to express future, these are: Will and Be Going to. They can be used for the following: Use will for:

  • Decisions now
  • Promises / offers / requests
  • Opinions Use be going to for:
  • Plans
  • Evidence

For the “be” part of this last phrase, we will use the 3 forms of the verb “be” that we also learned in the present: Am, Is and Are.

Affirmative Structures: #

WILL

Subject Will Verb (Base Form) Complement
I will drive a car next year.
You will run a marathon.
He will visit a new country.
She will study French tomorrow.
It will eat dog food.
We will drink chocolate.
You guys will write a letter.
They will read a novel next month.

BE GOING TO

Subject Be Going To Verb (Base Form) Complement
I am going to drive a car next year.
You are going to run a marathon.
He is going to visit a new country.
She is going to study French tomorrow.
It is going to eat dog food.
We are going to drink chocolate.
You guys are going to write a letter.
They are going to read a novel next month.

Negative Structures: #

WILL

Subject Will + Not Verb (Base Form) Complement
I won’t drive a car next year.
You won’t run a marathon.
He won’t visit a new country.
She won’t study French tomorrow.
It won’t eat dog food.
We won’t drink chocolate.
You guys won’t write a letter.
They won’t read a novel next month.

BE GOING TO

Subject Be Not Going To Verb (Base Form) Complement
I am not going to drive a car next year.
You aren’t going to run a marathon.
He isn’t going to visit a new country.
She isn’t going to study French tomorrow.
It isn’t going to eat dog food.
We aren’t going to drink chocolate.
You guys aren’t going to write a letter.
They aren’t going to read a novel next month.

NOTE: Please notice that the only thing that changes between affirmative and negative sentences is the “not”. In the chart above we used contractions but you can also use the long forms which are: “will not” and “am/are/is not going to”.

Question Structures: #

WILL

Will Subject Verb (Base Form) Complement?
Will I drive a car next year?

Will he visit a new country?
Will she study French tomorrow?
Will it eat dog food?
Will we drink chocolate?
Will you guys write a letter?
Will they read a novel next month?

BE GOING TO

Verb Be Subject Going To Verb (Base Form) Complement?
Am I going to drive a car next year?
Are you going to run a marathon?
Is he going to visit a new country?
Is she going to study French tomorrow?
Is it going to eat dog food?
Are we going to drink chocolate?
Are you guys going to write a letter?

You can also add Question words to these question structures to make them open-ended. Example:

  • Why are you guys going to write a letter? – It’s asking for the reason
  • Where are you going to run the marathon? – It’s asking for the place

Prepositions of time (Review): #

We use at, on, and in to talk about time.

AT: Use AT before specific hours. Examples:

  • I am going to wake up at 7:00.
  • The class will start at 9:30.
  • The meeting will be at noon (12pm).

👉 Think: AT = exact time

ON: Use ON for days of the week and dates. Examples:

  • I will work on Monday.
  • She’s going to have a test on Friday.
  • My birthday will be on July 10th.
  • We will have a party on Saturday night.

👉 Think: ON = a day

IN: Use IN for months, years, seasons and parts of the day. Examples:

  • My birthday will be in July.
  • I was born in 2000.
  • We are going to study in the morning.
  • She will work in the afternoon.

👉 Think: IN = a period of time

⚠️ Important Exception: #

❌ In the night → ✔️ at night #

Type Will Be going to
Affirmative I will study I’m going to study
Negative I won’t study I’m not going to study
Question Will you study? Are you going to study?

Key Tips: #

  • “Will” → no auxiliary verb needed (just will)
  • “Going to” → always needs verb “be” (am/is/are)
  • Main verb is always in base form
