Learning Objectives:

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  1. Identify and communicate dates and schedules clearly by using days of the week, months, ordinal numbers, and years.
  2. Describe and understand weather conditions and temperatures in order to respond to customer inquiries related to deliveries, or service interruptions caused by weather.
  3. Accurately read and state dates and years when verifying customer information, explaining billing periods, or scheduling future services.

Introduction: #

Today you will learn how to talk about time, dates, and weather in English, which are important skills for call center agents. You will practice using days of the week, months, ordinal numbers, and years to correctly say and confirm dates with customers.

You will also learn how to describe weather and temperatures, which can be useful when explaining service conditions or delivery delays.

By the end of the lesson, you will be able to communicate dates, schedules, and weather information clearly and professionally with customers.

Key Concepts(Grammar Rules) #

The Temperature:

To talk about temperature in English, we usually say the number + degrees + the temperature scale (Celsius or Fahrenheit).

Structure

Number + Degrees + Celcius/Fahrenheit

Examples:

  • 25°C → Twenty-five degrees Celsius
  • 30°C → Thirty degrees Celsius
  • 72°F → Seventy-two degrees Fahrenheit
  • 90°F → Ninety degrees Fahrenheit

Note: In the United States, people usually use Fahrenheit, in most other countries, people use Celsius.

Dates: In American English, dates are usually said using the month + the ordinal number of the day + the year. Keep in mind that the month goes first unlike the Spanish language where we usually say the day first.

Structure: MM + DD + YYYY

Examples:

  • March 1st, 2001 → March first, two thousand one
  • April 10th, 1996 → April tenth, nineteen ninety-six
  • July 23rd, 1905 → July twenty-third, nineteen oh five
  • December 31st, 1900 → December thirty-first, nineteen hundred

Note: We ONLY use ordinal numbers for the day (first, second, third, fourth, etc.) avoid using cardinal numbers.

How to Read Years in English:

  1. Years that end in 00 are read as “hundreds.” Examples
    • 1800 → eighteen hundred
    • 1900 → nineteen hundred
  2. Years from 1910 to 1999 are usually read in pairs. Example:
    • 1996 → nineteen ninety-six
    • 1984 → nineteen eighty-four
  3. Years from 2000 to 2009 are usually read as a full number. Examples:
    • 2003 → two thousand three
    • 2009 → two thousand nine
  4. From 2010 to the present, years are commonly read in pairs again. Examples:
    • 2013 → twenty thirteen
    • 2026 → twenty twenty-six

✔️ Tip: Both two thousand thirteen and twenty thirteen are correct, but “twenty thirteen” is more common in conversation. #

📝 Summary: #

Use the following structure to read a date: MM – DD – YYYY

  1. The month goes first.
  2. The day is read with ordinal numbers only.
  3. Each year has its own reading structure, memorizing works better in this case.
