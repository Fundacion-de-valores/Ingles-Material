Topics of the Day: #

  • Frequency adverbs
  • Daily Routine

Learning Objectives: #

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  1. Describe your daily routine and habits using frequency adverbs and expressions (e.g., always, usually, sometimes), to get ready to explain during a job interview how you manage responsibilities.
  2. Talk about your hobbies and interests to build rapport with interviewers and participate in small talk in a professional setting.
  3. Ask and answer questions about your routine and hobbies, as well as how often activities occur.

Introduction: #

Today, you will learn how to talk about your daily routine, hobbies, and traditions, and how to use frequency adverbs and expressions such as always, usually, sometimes, and never to describe how often you do things. This will help you talk about your habits and personal activities clearly.

After that, you will practice describing your daily activities and free-time interests, which will be of great importance in job related conversations.

Finally, you will use what you learned to structure questions in the simple present correctly.

Key Concepts(Grammar Rules) Frequency Adverbs:

Ley Concepts(Grammar Rules): #

Frequency Adverbs:

Frequency adverbs are words we use to talk about how often an action happens. They describe the frequency of an activity or habit. Common frequency adverbs include: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, and never.

Position in the sentence:

  1. Before the main verb: Frequency adverbs usually come before the main verb. Example:
    • I usually drink coffee in the morning.
    • She often studies at night.
  2. After the verb “to be”: When the verb to be (am, is, are) is used, the frequency adverb comes after it. Example:
    • I am always on time.
    • They are usually very busy.

Hobbies:

When we talk about the things we or other people like to do in their free time (hobbies), we often use gerunds and infinitives. These are verb forms that can function like nouns in a sentence. We usually use them after a main verb when we want to talk about another action.

  1. Gerunds: Verbs that end in -ing. Example: Reading.
  2. Infinitives: The preposition “to” plus a verb. Example: To read

You can use “like” or “love” as the main verb in your sentences about hobbies, followed by a gerund or infinitive. Example:

✔️ They love playing videogames. #

✔️ I like to read. #

Important Note: In English, we cannot use two verbs together in a sentence. When a verb is followed by another action, the second verb must be in the form of a gerund (-ing) or an infinitive (to + verb).

❌❌❌ I like read. #

✔️ I like reading. / I like to read. #

This is why we use gerunds and infinitives when talking about hobbies and activities.

Interrogative Sentences in Simple Present:

We use interrogative sentences to ask questions. As you learned yesterday, in English, we usually need an auxiliary verb to make questions.

Remember that in the Simple Present, the auxiliaries are:

  • Do (I, you, we, you guys, they)
  • Does (he / she / it)

Basic Question Structures:

Close Ended Questions Open Ended Questions
Aux Subject Verb Complement? Question Word Aux Verb Complement?
Do you need help? why do you need help?
Does she listen to music? where does listen to music?
Important Note: #

Unlike the affirmative and negative sentence structures, the questions do NOT start with the subject, in this case, the auxiliary will always go before the subject.

📝 Summary: #

Frequency Adverbs come before the main verb if it’s an action verb, but they come after the verb “be”.

Use gerunds (-ing) or infinitives (to + verb) after a main verb to talk about your hobbies.

Important Reminder: Two verbs cannot go together unless the second verb is a gerund or an infinitive.

❌ I love sing. #

✔️ I love singing / I love to sing. #
