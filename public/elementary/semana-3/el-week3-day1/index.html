Topics of the day #

  • Simple Past Rules and Uses

Learning Objectives: #

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  1. Describe past actions or interactions by using affirmative and negative sentences in the simple past.
  2. Use common simple past verbs to explain completed actions during customer service calls (Example: processed requests, handled complaints, updated accounts).
  3. Accurately pronounce regular past tense verb endings (-ed) to ensure clear and professional communication over the phone.

Introduction: #

Today, you will learn how to talk about past situations in a call center. You will practice how to form sentences to describe what happened during a call, including things you did and things you didn’t do. You will also learn common action words in their past form to explain tasks like helping customers, solving problems, and updating information. Finally, you will practice how to pronounce regular verb endings correctly, so your speech is clear and professional when speaking with customers. Important terms (Vocabulary):

Key Concepts (Grammar Rules): #

Simple Past Rules and Uses:

We use the simple past to talk about actions that started and finished in the past.

  • Completed actions: Used for things that happened and are finished. Example: I helped the customer yesterday.
  • Sequences of actions: Used to describe steps in a process or what happened during a call. Example: I answered the call, checked the account, and solved the issue.
  • Past time references: Often used with expressions like yesterday, last night, last week, ago. Example: She updated the system two hours ago.
  • Past situations (now finished): Used for states or situations that are no longer true. Example: The customer was upset, but now they are satisfied.
Affirmative Sentences Negative Seentences
I asked a question. I did not(didn’t) study for the test.
You bought a car. You didn’t play soccer last night.
He called his girlfriend. He did not run a marathon last year.
She drank tea yesterday. She didn’t sleep yesterday.
It ate my homework. It did not work properly.
We felt sick last week. We didn’t talk to Lea this morning.
You guys gave me a present. You guys did not win the contest.
They liked pizza but not anymore. They didn’t want to practice English.
Shirley paid her tuition. Johnny did not lose his card.
Mom and Dad told my brother to study. My friends didn’t watch that movie.

IMPORTANT: When we use an auxiliary, the verb will always be written in its base form, for the simple past, we use the auxiliary “did” and it is used with all of the pronouns (I, you, he, she, it, we, you guys and they).

Common Mistake: #

❌ I didn’t helped the customer. #

✔️ I didn’t help the customer. #

Regular Verbs Pronunciation: #

  • If the regular past verb ends in: -ped, -ked, -sed, -ched, -shed, -fed, -xed, or -hed, we pronounce the -ED as a “T”. Examples:

    • Asked (Pronunciation: Askt)
    • Brushed (Pronunciation: Brusht)
    • Dressed (Pronunciation: Dresst)
    • Dropped (Pronunciation: Droppt)
    • Fixed (Pronunciation: Fixt)

  • If the regular past verb ends in: -led, -ved, - ned, -med, -red, -bed, -ged, -wed, -yed, -zed or a vowel sound, we pronounce the -ED as “D”. Examples:

    • Allowed (Pronunciation: Allowd)
    • Burned (Pronunciation: Burnd)
    • Called (Pronunciation: Calld)
    • Covered (Pronunciation: Coverd)

  • If the regular past verb ends in: -Ted or -Ded. We pronounce the -ED as “ID”. Examples:

    • Accepted (Pronunciation: Acceptid)
    • Afforded (Pronunciation: Affordid)

Use the following link to find a great amount of regular verbs divided into their 3 pronunciation groups: https://www.thoughtco.com/past-tense-regular-verb-pronunciation-1210045

MASTER TIP: #

ONLY when the verb ends in TED or DED use the “ID” sound. For all other verbs, when in doubt, simple pronounce a “T” sound instead of “ed”.

📝 Summary: #

Affirmative: Subject + past verb (-ed / irregular). Example: I helped the customer. Negative: Subject + did not (didn’t) + base verb. Example: I didn’t help the customer.

✔️ Remember: In negatives, the verb goes back to base form, and pronunciation helps you sound clear and professional. #

Regular Verbs Pronunciation (-ed)

/t/ → after voiceless sounds (helped, worked)

/d/ → after voiced sounds (called, answered)

/ɪd/ → after /t/ or /d/ (needed, updated)
