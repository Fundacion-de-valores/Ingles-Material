Day 3 — Week 2 #

🎯 Learning Objectives #

At the end of the class, you will be able to:

  • ✅ Use possessive adjectives to indicate belonging or ownership to a person, animal, or thing.
  • ✅ Use possessive pronouns to replace a possessive adjective + noun and avoid repetition.
  • ✅ Form a possessive noun by adding ’s (or ’) to show ownership.

📖 Introduction #

Today you will use possessive adjectives, pronouns, and nouns to show ownership or a close relationship to someone or something.

📚 Important Terms — Daily Vocabulary #

🔵 1. Possessive Adjectives #

Possessive adjectives show possession or ownership. Each subject pronoun has its own possessive adjective:

Subject Pronoun Possessive Adjective Example
I my I have a car. My car is very old.
You your You have a book. Your book is new.
He his He has a dog. His dog is black.
She her She has a boyfriend. Her boyfriend is friendly.
It its It has a bone. Its bone is old.
We our We have a dog. Our dog is black.
They their They had homework. Their homework is on the table.

💡 Key rules:

  • Always placed directly before the noun → (Possessive Adjective + Noun)
  • Must agree with the possessor, not with the thing possessed
  • Never add -s when the noun is plural

Singular vs. Plural — the adjective never changes:

Singular Plural
✅ Correct My pen is black. My pens are black.
✅ Correct Our child is intelligent. Our children are intelligent.
✅ Correct Our car is expensive. Our cars are expensive.
❌ Wrong Ours cars are expensive.

⚠️ Its vs. It’s — Don’t confuse them! #

Form Meaning Example
Its Possessive adjective for it The cat licked its paw.
It’s Contraction of it is It’s a beautiful day.

🟢 2. Possessive Pronouns #

A possessive pronoun replaces a possessive adjective + noun to avoid repeating information that is already clear:

This book is my book, not your book. → This book is mine, not yours.

Subject Pronoun Possessive Adjective Possessive Pronoun
I my mine
You your yours
He his his
She her hers
It its (no possessive pronoun)
We our ours
You your yours
They their theirs

⚠️ IT has no possessive pronoun — never use its as a possessive pronoun.

Possessive Adjective vs. Possessive Pronoun — side by side:

Possessive Adjective Possessive Pronoun
My shirt is green. The shirt is mine.
Your book is new. The book is yours.
His pillow is soft. The pillow is his.
Her dog is small. The dog is hers.
Our bird is noisy. The bird is ours.
Your house is big. The house is yours.
Their car is slow. The car is theirs.

💡 Possessive pronouns are usually written at the end of the sentence.

🟡 3. Possessive Nouns #

A possessive noun shows ownership, possession, or a close relationship by adding ’s or just ’ to a noun.

Rule 1 — Singular Nouns: add ’s #

Instead of… Use…
The house of Peter Peter’s house
The bone of the dog The dog’s bone
The bike of your friend Your friend’s bike

Rule 2 — Plural Nouns ending in -s: add only ’ (apostrophe) #

Instead of… Use…
The permission of your parents Your parents’ permission
The bones of the dogs The dogs’ bones
The notebooks of my friends My friends’ notebooks

Rule 3 — Singular Nouns ending in -s: add ’s #

Instead of… Use…
The teacher of the class The class’s teacher
The office of my boss My boss’s office
The dress of the princess The princess’s dress

Rule 4 — Plural Nouns NOT ending in -s: add ’s #

Instead of… Use…
The grammar of the people People’s grammar
The toilet of the men The men’s toilet
The camping trip of the children The children’s camping trip

Rule 5 — Proper Nouns (Names) ending in -s: add ’s or just ’ #

Both are accepted
James’ dog is hungry. / James’s dog is hungry.
Carlos’ house is big. / Carlos’s house is big.

Rule 6 — Two or more people #

Situation Rule Example
Same object, shared owners Add ’s only to the last name Stacy and Steve’s car is old. (one car, both own it)
Different objects, different owners Add ’s to each name Stacy’s and Steve’s cars are old. (each has their own car)

💡 Extra Tip — No noun after the apostrophe #

When the object is already known, you don’t need to repeat it:

A: Whose umbrella is this? B: It’s Mary’s. ← No need to say “Mary’s umbrella” again.

⚠️ Important — Inanimate objects #

Do NOT use possessive ’s with inanimate objects. Use an adjective or “of” phrase instead:

❌ Avoid ✅ Use instead
The desk’s drawer is stuck. The desk drawer is stuck.
The table’s leg is broken. The leg of the table is broken.

🔑 Key Concepts — Quick Reference #

Possessive Adjectives #

  • ✅ Use each adjective with its corresponding subject pronoun
  • ✅ Always placed before the noun
  • ❌ Never used in plural form
  • ⚠️ Don’t confuse its (possessive) with it’s (it is)

Possessive Pronouns #

  • ✅ Use at the end of the sentence
  • ✅ Replace possessive adjective + noun to avoid repetition
  • ⚠️ IT has no possessive pronoun

Possessive Nouns #

  • ✅ Add ’s for singular nouns
  • ✅ Add only ’ for plural nouns ending in -s
  • ❌ Do not use with inanimate objects — use “of” instead

💡 Examples #

Possessive Adjectives #

“I have a car. My car is very old.” “She has a boyfriend. Her boyfriend is very friendly.” “We have a dog. Our dog is black.”

Possessive Pronouns #

“This book is mine, not yours.” “The pillow is his. The dog is hers.” “The car is theirs.”

Possessive Nouns #

“We went to Peter’s house last night.” “You need your parents’ permission.” "Stacy and Steve’s car is old."

✏️ Practical Exercises #

Exercise 1 — Fill in the Possessive Adjective #

Complete with the correct possessive adjective:

“I have a pen. ___ pen is blue.” → My “She has a cat. ___ cat is white.” → Her “They have a house. ___ house is big.” → Their “We have children. ___ children are smart.” → Our

Exercise 2 — Rewrite using Possessive Pronouns #

Replace the bold part with a possessive pronoun:

“This is my book.” → “This book is ___.” → mine “That is her car.” → “That car is ___.” → hers “These are their shoes.” → “These shoes are ___.” → theirs “That is our dog.” → “That dog is ___.” → ours

Exercise 3 — Form the Possessive Noun #

Rewrite using possessive ’s or ‘:

“The room of Carlos” → Carlos’s / Carlos’ room “The toys of the children” → The children’s toys “The car of my parents” → My parents’ car “The office of my boss” → My boss’s office

Exercise 4 — Speaking Activity #

Describe 3 things that belong to you or someone you know:

  1. Use a possessive adjective: “My sister’s phone is new.”
  2. Use a possessive pronoun: “That phone is hers.”
  3. Use a possessive noun: “It’s Maria’s phone.”

🎙️ Record yourself and share with the class!

📝 Summary — Key Points to Remember #

Before you go, make sure you know:

  1. Possessive Adjectives — my, your, his, her, its, our, your, their → always before the noun, never in plural.
  2. Possessive Pronouns — mine, yours, his, hers, ours, yours, theirs → at the end of the sentence. (No possessive pronoun for IT.)
  3. Possessive Nouns — Add ’s (singular) / ’ (plural ending in -s). Do not use with inanimate objects.
  4. Its vs. It’s — Its = belonging to it / It’s = it is.

Keep practicing every day — little by little you will sound more natural! 🌟
