🎯 Learning Objectives #

At the end of the class, you will be able to:

  • ✅ Compare different people, animals, things, or places using comparative adjectives.
  • ✅ Explain the difference between two nouns using the correct comparative form.
  • ✅ Express equality between two nouns using as + adjective + as.
  • ✅ Highlight one outstanding noun from a group using superlative adjectives.

📖 Introduction #

Today you will learn how to use comparative and superlative adjectives to compare people, animals, things, or places that are different — and to express when they are the same. You will also learn how to describe one outstanding noun that stands out from a group. These structures are essential for everyday conversations, descriptions, and professional communication.

📚 Important Terms — Daily Vocabulary #

Term Definition
Comparative Adjective Used to compare two nouns and show a difference (bigger, more interesting)
Comparative of Equality Used to say two nouns are the same in some way (as tall as)
Superlative Adjective Used to highlight one outstanding noun from a group (the biggest, the most interesting)
Than Used after a comparative adjective (She is taller than me.)
The Always used before a superlative adjective (She is the tallest.)
Irregular Adjectives Adjectives that change completely (good → better → the best)

🔑 Key Concepts — Detailed Explanation #

🔵 1. Comparative Adjectives of Equality #

Use as + adjective + as to say two things are the same in some way. Use not as + adjective + as to talk about a difference.

Structure Example
as + adj + as Bogotá is as interesting as Buenos Aires.
not as + adj + as The two-star hotel isn’t as expensive as the four-star hotel.

🟢 2. Comparative Adjectives — Rules #

Use comparative adjectives to show change or make comparisons between 2 nouns. Use than when comparing one thing to another.

Rule 1 — 1-syllable adjectives: add -er #

Adjective Comparative Example
small smaller My city is smaller than yours.
big bigger The dog is bigger than the cat.
tall taller Henry is taller than Mike.
nice nicer That car is nicer than the other one.

Rule 2 — 2-syllable adjectives ending in -y: drop -y, add -ier #

Adjective Comparative Example
pretty prettier My mom is prettier than his.
crazy crazier Cats are crazier than dogs.
lazy lazier Sophie is lazier than Ana.
friendly friendlier I am friendlier than your sister.

Rule 3 — 2+ syllable adjectives: use more #

Adjective Comparative Example
serious more serious Jack is more serious than Nikki.
important more important Is math more important than music?
intelligent more intelligent Kacey is more intelligent than Brenda.

Rule 4 — Irregular adjectives #

Adjective Comparative Example
good better Soccer is better than basketball.
bad worse Hamburgers are worse than pizza.
far farther / further Brazil is farther than Panamá.
little (quantity) less My city is less cold than yours.

💡 Less works like more — same structure: Gary is less handsome than Tom. Crissy is less intelligent than Mary.

🟡 3. Superlative Adjectives — Rules #

Use superlative adjectives to compare three or more nouns and highlight one outstanding noun from the group. Always use the before the superlative.

Rule 1 — 1-syllable adjectives: add -est #

Adjective Superlative Example
small the smallest My city is the smallest in my country.
big the biggest The black dog is the biggest.
tall the tallest Henry is the tallest boy in his class.
nice the nicest Greg is the nicest person I know.

Rule 2 — 2-syllable adjectives ending in -y: drop -y, add -iest #

Adjective Superlative Example
pretty the prettiest My mom is the prettiest in the family.
crazy the craziest Milo is the craziest cat in the neighborhood.
lazy the laziest My dog is the laziest animal in the world.
friendly the friendliest Melody is the friendliest person in the class.

Rule 3 — 2+ syllable adjectives: use the most #

Adjective Superlative Example
serious the most serious Jack is the most serious person at work.
important the most important Is English the most important subject?
intelligent the most intelligent Kacey is the most intelligent woman I know.

Rule 4 — Irregular adjectives #

Adjective Superlative Example
good the best Soccer is the best sport.
bad the worst Hamburgers are the worst fast food.
far the farthest / furthest Argentina is the farthest country in South America.
little (quantity) the least My city is the least cold in the country.

💡 The least works like the most — same structure: Gary is the least handsome guy in my class. Crissy is the least intelligent student here.

💡 Examples #

Equality #

“Ana is as intelligent as Nancy.” “This hotel is not as expensive as the other one.”

Comparative #

“I am shorter than my father.” “Burger King is better than McDonald’s.”

Superlative #

“Sushi is the most delicious food for me.” “My neighbors are the noisiest people I know.” “Snow White is the worst Disney movie.”

✏️ Practical Exercises #

Exercise 1 — Comparative of Equality #

Complete with as … as or not as … as:

Sentence Answer
Bogotá is ___ interesting ___ Buenos Aires. (same) Bogotá is as interesting as Buenos Aires.
The budget hotel is ___ expensive ___ the luxury hotel. (different) It is not as expensive as the luxury hotel.

Exercise 2 — Comparative Adjectives #

Write the comparative form of each adjective:

Adjective Comparative
big ________
pretty ________
intelligent ________
good ________
bad ________

(Answers: bigger / prettier / more intelligent / better / worse)

Exercise 3 — Superlative Adjectives #

Write the superlative form of each adjective:

Adjective Superlative
tall ________
lazy ________
important ________
good ________
bad ________

(Answers: the tallest / the laziest / the most important / the best / the worst)

Exercise 4 — Complete the Sentences #

Fill in the blank with the correct comparative or superlative form:

“New York is ___ (big) than Boston.” → bigger “She is ___ (intelligent) student in the class.” → the most intelligent “This is ___ (bad) movie I have ever seen.” → the worst “He is ___ (friendly) than his brother.” → friendlier

Exercise 5 — Speaking Practice #

Compare these pairs using comparative and superlative adjectives:

  1. Two cities you know → "___ is bigger than ___, but ___ is the biggest city in ___."
  2. Two foods you like → "___ is better than ___, but ___ is the best."
  3. Two people you know → "___ is more ___ than ___, but ___ is the most ___ person I know."

📝 Summary — Key Points to Remember #

Before you go, make sure you know:

  1. Equality — as + adjective + as (same) / not as + adjective + as (different).
  2. Comparative Adjectives — Compare 2 nouns; use -er (short), more (long), or irregular forms; always use than.
  3. Superlative Adjectives — Highlight 1 noun from a group; use -est (short), the most (long), or irregular forms; always use the.
  4. Irregular Forms — good → better → the best / bad → worse → the worst / far → farther → the farthest / little → less → the least.
Comparative Superlative
1-syllable + -er + -est
-y ending drop -y + -ier drop -y + -iest
2+ syllables more + adj the most + adj
Irregular better / worse / farther / less the best / the worst / the farthest / the least
