🎯 Learning Objectives #

At the end of the class, you will be able to:

  • ✅ Use can to talk about ability, permission, possibility, and making requests in the present.
  • ✅ Use could to talk about past ability, permission, possibility, and making polite requests.
  • ✅ Differentiate between can and could in questions, statements, and negative forms.
  • ✅ Apply can and could in real-life situations such as making requests, giving permission, describing abilities, or politely asking for help.

📖 Introduction #

Today you will learn and practice can and could to talk about ability, permission, possibility, and requests. You will focus on how to describe what you or others are able to do, ask for help politely, and give or deny permission in real-life situations. As an A1 student, mastering these structures will help you communicate effectively in contexts such as work, school, customer service, or daily conversations.

📚 Important Terms — Daily Vocabulary #

Term Definition
Can (Ability) I know how to do it — I can swim.
Can (Permission) It is allowed — You can use my pen.
Can (Request) Asking politely (informal) — Can you help me?
Could (Past Ability) Was able to do it before — When I was young, I could run fast.
Could (Polite Request) More formal / polite — Could you open the window, please?
Could (Permission) Politely asking if allowed — Could I borrow your notebook?
Could (Possibility) Something might happen — It could rain later.

🔑 Key Concepts — Detailed Explanation #

🔵 1. Can — Present Ability / Permission / Requests #

Use Example
Ability I can swim. / She can speak three languages.
Permission You can borrow my notebook. / He can leave early.
Request (informal) Can you help me? / Can I ask a question?

🟢 2. Could — Past Ability / Polite Requests / Permission / Possibility #

Use Example
Past ability When I was young, I could run very fast.
Polite request Could you open the window, please?
Permission (polite) Could I borrow your notebook?
Possibility It could rain later.

💡 Could is more formal and polite than can for requests. Use could in professional or formal situations.

🟡 3. Structure #

Form Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + can/could + base verb I can speak English.
Negative Subject + can’t / couldn’t + base verb She can’t come today.
Interrogative Can/Could + subject + base verb? Can you help me? / Could I ask a question?

⚠️ Never add “to” after can/could: ❌ I can to help you. → ✅ I can help you.

🟠 4. Polite Expressions for Requests #

More informal → More formal / polite →
Can you help me? Could you help me, please?
Can I use your phone? Could I use your phone for a moment?

🎙️ Tip: Use rising intonation at the end of polite requests to sound natural and friendly.

💡 Examples #

Ability — Can / Could #

“I can swim.” “She can speak three languages.” “When I was young, I could run very fast.”

Permission — Can / Could #

“You can borrow my notebook.” “He can leave early today.” "Could I open the window?" (polite)

Requests — Can / Could #

"Can you help me with this exercise?" "Could you pass me the salt, please?" "Can I ask you a question?" "Could I use your phone for a moment?"

Negative Forms #

“I can’t swim very well.” “She can’t come to the meeting today.” “He couldn’t finish the homework yesterday.”

Combined — Real-life #

“I can help you with your homework. Could you bring me your notebook?” “She can speak English, and he can’t. Could you explain that again, please?” “I can’t come to the party tonight. Could I join next time?”

✏️ Practical Exercises #

Exercise 1 — Ability (Can / Could) #

Choose the correct modal verb:

Sentence Answer
I ___ (can / could) play the guitar. I can play the guitar. (present)
She ___ (can / could) speak three languages. She can speak three languages.
When I was younger, I ___ (can / could) run very fast. I could run very fast. (past)

🎯 Tell a partner 3 things you can do now and 2 things you could do when you were younger.

Exercise 2 — Permission (Can / Could) #

Complete the sentences:

Sentence Answer
You ___ (can / could) borrow my pen. You can borrow my pen.
He ___ (can / could) leave early today. He can leave early today.
___ (Can / Could) I open the window, please? Could I open the window, please? (polite)

🎯 Ask your partner for 3 permissions politely using can or could.

Exercise 3 — Requests (Can / Could) #

Write polite requests:

Prompt Your request
Ask for help with an exercise. Can / Could you help me with this exercise?
Ask to use someone’s phone. Can / Could I use your phone for a moment?
Ask someone to pass the salt. Could you pass me the salt, please?

🎯 Role-play with a partner — one person asks politely, the other responds with “Yes, of course!” or “I’m sorry, I can’t.”

Exercise 4 — Negative Forms #

Complete the sentences with can’t or couldn’t:

Sentence Answer
I ___ swim very well. I can’t swim very well. (present)
She ___ come to the meeting today. She can’t come today. (present)
He ___ finish the homework yesterday. He couldn’t finish yesterday. (past)

🎯 Talk with a partner about things you cannot do now and things you couldn’t do in the past.

Exercise 5 — Speaking Practice (Combined) #

Topic: Helping others / Asking for permission

Use:

  • 2 sentences about your abilities using can
  • 2 polite requests using can or could

Example: “I can help you with your homework. Could you bring me your notebook?”

📝 Summary — Key Points to Remember #

Before you go, make sure you know:

  1. Ability — Can = present ability (I can swim) / Could = past ability (I could run fast).
  2. Permission — Can = give/deny permission (You can use my pen) / Could = polite permission (Could I borrow your notebook?).
  3. Requests — Can you…? / Could you…? → asking for help; Can I…? / Could I…? → asking for permission. Use rising intonation for polite requests.
  4. Structure — Affirmative: subject + can/could + base verb. Negative: subject + can’t/couldn’t + base verb. Question: Can/Could + subject + base verb?

⚠️ Common mistakes to avoid: ❌ I can to help you. → ✅ I can help you. ❌ Could I can borrow your pen? → ✅ Could I borrow your pen? ❌ She can’t to swim. → ✅ She can’t swim.
