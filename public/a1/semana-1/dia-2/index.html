🎯 Learning Objectives #

At the end of the class, you will be able to:

  • ✅ Master Simple Present with “to be” (am/is/are) and other verbs for descriptions, habits, and routines — in affirmative, negative, and question forms.
  • ✅ Use “to be” + adjectives correctly to describe people and things.
  • ✅ Apply possessive adjectives to show ownership properly.

📖 Introduction #

Today you will describe people and things using adjectives and the verb “to be” correctly. You will talk about daily habits and truths with other verbs. Finally, you will show ownership using possessives for real-life conversations like work or family talk.

📚 Important Terms — Daily Vocabulary #

Term Definition
Simple Present (Verb To Be) am / is / are for descriptions, professions, age, nationalities → “I am tall.”
Simple Present (Other Verbs) Base verb for habits, routines, or truths → “She walks.”
Verb To Be + Adjectives Links subject to qualities after am/is/are or before nouns → “He is tall” / “tall man”
Possessive Adjectives my, your, his, her, its, our, their — show belonging before nouns
Auxiliary Verbs Do = I, we, they, you / Does = she, it, he (for other verbs) — no auxiliary needed for “be” negatives/questions

🔑 Key Concepts — Detailed Explanation #

🔵 1. Simple Present — Verb To Be #

Form Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + to be + Adjective I am intelligent.
Negative Subject + to be + not + Adjective She is not happy.
Interrogative To be + Subject + Complement Are they hungry?

Contractions you can use:

Full form Contraction
I am not I’m not
She is not She’s not / isn’t
They are not They’re not / aren’t

Short Answers:

Question Yes No
Are they hungry? Yes, they are. No, they aren’t.
Is she tired? Yes, she is. No, she isn’t.

🟢 2. Simple Present — Other Verbs #

Form Structure Example
Affirmative Subject + base verb + complement I eat breakfast in the morning.
Negative Subject + do/does + not + complement I don’t watch TV in the afternoon.
Interrogative Do/Does + subject + complement Do you have crayons in your backpack?

When to use Do / Does:

Auxiliary Subjects
Do I, you, we, they
Does He, she, it

💡 Remember: with does*, the main verb keeps its base form — no “s” added.* ✅ She does walk. / ❌ *She does walks.*

🟡 3. Verb To Be + Adjectives #

Adjectives can go in 2 positions:

Position Structure Example
Position 1 After “be” I am happy.
Position 2 Before noun a happy person

⚠️ Important rules:

  • Adjectives never use plural form → ❌ bigs apples ✅ big apples
  • Adjectives always describe nouns

🟠 4. Possessive Adjectives #

Match the possessive adjective to the owner:

Pronoun Possessive Example
I my my car
You your your bag
He his his book
She her her house
It its its color
We our our school
They their their cars

⚠️ Rules to remember:

  • Always use possessive adjectives before a noun → ✅ “my car”
  • ❌ “mine car” / ❌ “my is”
  • They do not change for the noun’s number or gender

💡 Examples #

Verb To Be #

“I am a teacher.” “She is not tired.” “Are they happy? — Yes, they are.”

Other Verbs #

“I play soccer.” “They don’t work late.” “Do you live here? — Yes, I do.”

Be + Adjectives #

“My cat is cute.” / “I have a cute cat.” “She is smart.” / “She is a smart student.”

Possessives #

“This is my book.” “Her house is big.” “Their cars are new.”

Combined #

“I am your student.” “My teacher is nice.” “She teaches every day.”

✏️ Practical Exercises #

Exercise 1 — Complete the sentences #

Fill in the blanks with the correct form:

“She ___ (be) tall.” → is "*** you *** (play) tennis? No, I ___." → Do / play / don’t

Exercise 2 — Adjective Position #

Rewrite the sentence correctly:

“He is good teacher.” → “He is a good teacher.”

Exercise 3 — Possessives #

Fill in the blanks:

“Is this ___ (you) bag?” → your “Yes, it’s ___ (I) pen.” → my

Exercise 4 — Speaking in Pairs #

Describe your partner using the structures learned:

  1. “You are ___ (adjective).”
  2. "___ (possessive) friend is nice."
  3. "*** (you) *** (verb) every day?"

📝 Summary — Key Points to Remember #

Before you go, make sure you know:

  1. Verb To Be — Use am / is / are for descriptions. Adjectives go after the verb to be or before nouns.
  2. Other Verbs — Use base form for I/you/they/we. Use do/does for negatives and questions.
  3. Possessives — my / your / his / her / its / our / their always go before a noun.
  4. Daily practice — “My family is happy. We eat breakfast together.”

Keep practicing every day — little by little you will sound more natural! 🌟
