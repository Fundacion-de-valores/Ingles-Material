🎯 Learning Objectives #

At the end of the class, you will be able to:

  • ✅ Pronounce and spell the English alphabet correctly.
  • ✅ Recognize and say numbers from 0 to 1,000,000 including teen/ten differences and TH sounds.
  • ✅ Use titles like Mr., Mrs., Ms. properly.
  • ✅ Apply basic connected speech rules for natural pronunciation.

📖 Introduction #

Today you will pronounce and spell words correctly following the Alphabet and the Military Phonetic Alphabet. Besides, you will recognize and write numbers from 1 to 1,000,000. You will be able to understand the differences of teen/ten and practice the TH sound. Finally, you will learn how to use titles properly and address people in a polite manner. You will also learn two basic rules of connected speech.

📚 Important Terms — Daily Vocabulary #

Term Definition
Alphabet 26 letters (A to Z) used to spell words and names.
Military Alphabet Clear code like Alpha (A), Bravo (B) for spelling over the phone.
Numbers 0–1,000,000. Watch: teen (13) vs. ten (10), and TH in three / thirteen / thirty.
Titles Mr. (man), Mrs. (married woman), Ms. (woman), Miss (single woman), Ma’am, Sir.
Connected Speech Linking words naturally: Consonant + Vowel → “put it” → “putit” / Consonant + Consonant → “big grape” → “bigrape”.

Using the Military Alphabet with “AS IN”:

🔢 2. Numbers #

Range: 0 to 1,000,000

Key tips to remember:

Concept Examples
Teens come before tens thirteen (13), thirty-one (31)
TH sound /θ/ three, thirty, thirteen
Money reading $135 → “one thirty-five dollars” / “one hundred thirty-five dollars”

🎩 3. Titles #

Use these to address people politely and correctly:

Title Use
Mr. Any man (married or not)
Mrs. Married woman
Ms. Any woman (married or not)
Miss Single / unmarried woman
Sir Polite form for a man
Ma’am Polite form for a woman

🗣️ 4. Connected Speech #

Only 2 rules for today:

Rule Example Natural pronunciation
Consonant → Vowel “turn it” → “turnit”
Consonant → Consonant “good day” → “gooday”

💡 These links make your English sound more natural and fluent!

💡 Examples #

Alphabet #

Spell “John” with Military Code:

  • J as in Juliet
  • H as in Hotel
  • O as in Oscar
  • N as in November

Numbers #

  • 14 → fourteen / 40 → forty
  • $2,500 → “two thousand five hundred dollars”

Titles #

  • “Good morning, Mr. Lopez.”
  • “Hello, Ms. Ruiz.”

Connected Speech #

  • “Big apple” → “bigapple”
  • “What is it?” → “Wha-tis-it?”

✏️ Practical Exercises #

Exercise 1 — Spelling #

Write 5 classmates’ names and spell them aloud using the military phonetic code.

Exercise 2 — Numbers #

  • Say all numbers from 10 to 100 (by tens).
  • Then read aloud: $1,234.

Exercise 3 — Titles & Connected Speech #

  • Greet 3 different partners using the correct title:

    “Hi, Ms. [Name], turn on your camera.”

  • Link the words naturally.
  • Record yourself and listen back!

📝 Summary — Key Points to Remember #

Before you go, make sure you know:

  1. Alphabet & Military Code — Practice spelling names daily using the phonetic alphabet.
  2. Numbers — Know teens vs. tens, master the TH /θ/ sound, and read money amounts correctly (e.g., $135 → “one thirty-five”).
  3. Titles — Use Mr. / Mrs. / Ms. / Miss / Sir / Ma’am for polite and professional communication.
  4. Connected Speech — Link Consonant + Vowel and Consonant + Consonant to speak more naturally.

Keep practicing every day — little by little you will sound more natural! 🌟
